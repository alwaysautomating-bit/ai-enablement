# Detect Accidental Ossification

1. State the claimed reason the subject must not change.
2. Separate current consequence evidence from age, authority, familiarity, sunk cost, and documentation status.
3. Recover the assumptions and environment behind the original decision.
4. Test whether those assumptions still hold.
5. Identify obsolete constraints, circular justification, and unexamined institutional inertia.
6. Report `justified rigidity`, `possible ossification`, or `insufficient evidence`.

Do not argue for change merely because change is possible. Preserve valid safety and dependency constraints. A documented decision is evidence of prior reasoning, not a prohibition against future reasoning.
