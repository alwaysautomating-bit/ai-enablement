# Platform Voice Calibration

Platform fluency means sounding native to a channel without becoming a different author. A platform may change the container, pacing, structure, and emphasis. It must not become the primary source of voice or alter the underlying meaning.

## Precedence

Apply calibration in this order:

1. **Source truth and meaning:** preserve what is known, claimed, uncertain, and intended.
2. **Core voice:** preserve the author's identity, authority, vocabulary, and durable style constraints.
3. **Objective and stakes:** decide what the communication must accomplish and how much tonal freedom the subject permits.
4. **Audience and context:** account for reader knowledge, relationship, and situation.
5. **Platform delivery contract:** adapt length, formatting, pacing, opening, density, and interaction pattern.
6. **Optional mood or nuance:** apply a bounded tonal direction only when it remains compatible with every higher layer.

A lower layer never overrides a higher one. A request for a playful social post cannot make a serious safety disclosure playful. A platform convention cannot justify changing a fact or inventing certainty.

## Hard constraints and soft defaults

Separate platform requirements into two classes:

- **Hard constraints** are current technical or policy limits that must be verified when material: supported media, character limits, link behavior, accessibility, and publishing rules.
- **Soft defaults** are revisable writing heuristics: typical length, paragraph shape, hook intensity, pacing, whitespace, and call-to-action style.

Soft defaults are starting points, not platform truth. They should be tuned from observed results without turning performance patterns into rigid stereotypes or algorithm folklore.

## Calibration reasoning chain

1. Establish the source meaning and non-negotiable facts.
2. Load the core voice and identify its invariants.
3. Determine the objective, audience, and stakes.
4. Select the platform's delivery contract.
5. Apply an optional mood or custom nuance within the established boundaries.
6. Rewrite for the platform; do not merely truncate or reformat.
7. Test both cross-platform cohesion and platform-native fluency.
8. Route the output through human review when publication, reputation, or consequence warrants it.

## Two independent quality tests

**Identity cohesion:** Would a reader recognize the same author across every version? Are the authority, meaning, and durable voice constraints intact?

**Platform fluency:** Does each version fit how people consume information on that platform? Are length, layout, pacing, and emphasis suitable for the channel?

An output can pass one test and fail the other. Identical formatting across platforms may preserve identity but feel unnatural. Aggressive platform mimicry may feel native while erasing the author.

## Failure modes

- letting a platform profile replace the core voice
- treating a provisional word range as a permanent rule
- changing the meaning to manufacture a stronger hook
- applying humor or warmth when the stakes require precision
- copying one draft everywhere with only superficial shortening
- optimizing for imagined algorithms rather than the intended reader
- allowing a custom tone note to override evidence, safety, or brand invariants

## Governance boundary

The agent may propose calibrated drafts and explain its transformations. The human owner decides whether the platform profile represents the intended identity, whether the current technical constraints are accurate, and whether the output is ready to publish.
